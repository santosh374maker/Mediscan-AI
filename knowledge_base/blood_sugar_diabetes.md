# Blood Sugar and Diabetes Markers

## Fasting Glucose
Fasting glucose measures blood sugar after roughly 8 hours without food. A
fasting level under 100 mg/dL is considered normal, 100–125 mg/dL is
labeled "prediabetes" (impaired fasting glucose), and 126 mg/dL or above
on more than one occasion suggests diabetes.

## HbA1c (Glycated Haemoglobin)
HbA1c reflects average blood sugar over roughly the past two to three
months, because glucose attaches to haemoglobin in red blood cells over
their lifespan. An HbA1c below 5.7% is normal, 5.7–6.4% indicates
prediabetes, and 6.5% or higher on repeat testing indicates diabetes. Its
main advantage over a single fasting glucose reading is that it is not
affected by what someone ate the day before the test.

## Postprandial Glucose
This measures blood sugar roughly two hours after a meal. It is useful for
catching blood sugar spikes that a fasting test alone would miss, which
matters because post-meal spikes are an independent contributor to
long-term vascular damage.

## Why the combination matters more than any single value
Diabetes and prediabetes risk is rarely about one number in isolation.
Clinicians look at fasting glucose, HbA1c, and the lipid panel together,
because insulin resistance tends to produce a recognizable *pattern*:
mildly elevated glucose, elevated triglycerides, and low HDL cholesterol
often appear together even before any single value crosses into a
clearly abnormal range on its own. This clustering is sometimes referred
to as metabolic syndrome, and its presence raises cardiovascular risk
beyond what the sugar numbers alone would suggest.

## Lifestyle and monitoring notes
Regular physical activity, reducing refined carbohydrate and sugary drink
intake, weight management, and adequate sleep are the most consistently
evidence-backed ways to improve insulin sensitivity. People with
prediabetic ranges are usually advised to recheck levels periodically
rather than being immediately started on medication, though this decision
depends on the full clinical picture and should be made with a doctor.
