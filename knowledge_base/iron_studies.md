# Iron Studies

## Serum Iron
Serum iron measures the amount of iron currently circulating in the
blood, bound to a transport protein. It fluctuates through the day and
with recent diet, so it is rarely interpreted alone.

## Ferritin
Ferritin reflects the body's stored iron and is generally considered the
single most useful marker for diagnosing iron deficiency, since stores
are depleted before circulating iron or haemoglobin actually drop. Low
ferritin is a strong indicator of iron deficiency. Ferritin can, however,
also rise as part of a general inflammatory response, which can mask a
true underlying deficiency — a limitation worth knowing when interpreting
ferritin in someone who is acutely unwell or has a chronic inflammatory
condition.

## TIBC (Total Iron Binding Capacity)
TIBC measures the blood's overall capacity to bind and transport iron.
When the body is iron deficient, TIBC tends to rise (the body "wants" to
carry more iron per available carrier), so a high TIBC alongside low
serum iron and low ferritin is a classic combined pattern for iron
deficiency anaemia.

## Putting it together with the CBC
Iron studies are typically ordered alongside a CBC when haemoglobin is
low and a microcytic (small red cell, low MCV) anaemia pattern is seen.
The combination of low ferritin, low serum iron, high TIBC, and low
MCV/MCH together gives a much more confident diagnosis of iron deficiency
anaemia than any one of these values would alone.
