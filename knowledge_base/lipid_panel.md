# Lipid Panel (Cholesterol)

## Total Cholesterol
Total cholesterol is the sum of LDL, HDL, and a portion of triglycerides.
On its own it is a fairly blunt measure — two people with the same total
cholesterol can have very different heart disease risk depending on how
that total splits between LDL and HDL.

## LDL ("bad" cholesterol)
LDL carries cholesterol into artery walls, where it can contribute to
plaque buildup (atherosclerosis) over years. Lower LDL is generally
associated with lower cardiovascular risk, and target LDL levels are
often set more strictly for people who already have other cardiovascular
risk factors such as diabetes or high blood pressure.

## HDL ("good" cholesterol)
HDL helps carry cholesterol away from artery walls back to the liver for
processing. Unlike most other markers on a blood panel, higher HDL is
generally better, and low HDL is itself an independent cardiovascular
risk factor, even when LDL looks fine.

## Triglycerides
Triglycerides are the main form of stored fat and rise with excess
calorie intake (especially refined carbohydrates and alcohol), obesity,
and poorly controlled diabetes. Very high triglycerides also raise the
risk of pancreatitis, separate from cardiovascular risk.

## The pattern that matters most: the "lipid triad"
A specific combination — elevated triglycerides, low HDL, and a shift
toward smaller/denser LDL particles — is a well-recognized risk pattern
often seen together with insulin resistance and metabolic syndrome. This
is a good example of why cardiovascular risk assessment looks at the
relationships between lipid values (like the triglyceride-to-HDL ratio)
rather than any single number in isolation.
