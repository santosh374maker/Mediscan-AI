# Kidney Function Tests

## Creatinine
Creatinine is a waste product from normal muscle breakdown that healthy
kidneys filter out of the blood efficiently. When kidney filtering
capacity drops, creatinine builds up in the blood. Because muscle mass
affects baseline creatinine, reference ranges differ somewhat by sex, and
a single creatinine value is usually interpreted alongside eGFR rather
than alone.

## eGFR (estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate)
eGFR is a calculated estimate of how much blood the kidneys filter per
minute, derived from creatinine, age, and sex. An eGFR above 90 is
generally normal, 60–89 may reflect mild reduction (sometimes normal with
age), and below 60 for three months or more is a marker used to define
chronic kidney disease. eGFR is generally considered more clinically
useful than creatinine alone because it adjusts for individual factors
that affect baseline creatinine.

## Urea / BUN (Blood Urea Nitrogen)
Urea is another waste product filtered by the kidneys, generated from
protein breakdown. It rises with reduced kidney filtration but is also
affected by diet, hydration status, and gastrointestinal bleeding, making
it a less specific marker than creatinine or eGFR on its own.

## Uric Acid
Uric acid is a breakdown product of purines (found in certain foods and
produced by normal cell turnover). Elevated uric acid can cause gout
(painful joint inflammation, often in the big toe) and kidney stones, and
can also both contribute to and result from reduced kidney function.

## Why combining these markers matters
Creatinine, eGFR, urea, and uric acid each have blind spots when
considered alone — for instance, creatinine can look "normal" in early
kidney disease if muscle mass is unusually low. Looking at the combination,
along with the trend over repeat tests rather than a single reading, gives
a much clearer picture of kidney health than any one value in isolation.
