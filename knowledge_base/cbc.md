# Complete Blood Count (CBC)

## What it measures
A CBC counts and describes the cells that make up blood: red blood cells
(RBCs), white blood cells (WBCs), and platelets. It is one of the most
commonly ordered blood tests and gives a broad snapshot of general health.

## Haemoglobin and Haematocrit
Haemoglobin is the protein inside red blood cells that carries oxygen from
the lungs to the rest of the body. Haematocrit is the percentage of blood
volume made up of red cells. Low haemoglobin (anaemia) can cause fatigue,
pale skin, shortness of breath, and dizziness. Common causes include iron
deficiency, vitamin B12 or folate deficiency, chronic disease, or blood
loss. High haemoglobin can be seen in dehydration, smoking, or lung/heart
conditions that reduce blood oxygen levels.

## Red Blood Cell Indices (MCV, MCH)
MCV (mean corpuscular volume) describes the average size of red blood
cells. A low MCV (microcytic) often points to iron deficiency or
thalassaemia. A high MCV (macrocytic) is often linked to vitamin B12 or
folate deficiency, or alcohol use. MCH reflects the average amount of
haemoglobin per red blood cell and tends to move in the same direction as
MCV.

## White Blood Cells (WBC)
White blood cells are the immune system's front line. A high WBC count
(leukocytosis) often signals infection, inflammation, or stress on the
body, and in rare cases can indicate a blood disorder. A low WBC count
(leukopenia) can result from viral infections, certain medications, or
bone marrow problems, and may mean reduced ability to fight infection.

## Neutrophils and Lymphocytes
Neutrophils are usually the largest share of white blood cells and respond
quickly to bacterial infections. Lymphocytes fight viral infections and
help produce antibodies. The relative balance between the two can help a
doctor distinguish a likely bacterial versus viral cause of illness.

## Platelets
Platelets help blood clot. A low platelet count (thrombocytopenia)
increases bleeding and bruising risk. A high platelet count
(thrombocytosis) can increase clotting risk and may be reactive (from
inflammation or iron deficiency) or, less commonly, linked to a bone
marrow disorder.

## When to seek care
Very low haemoglobin, very low platelets, or a very high or very low WBC
count found alongside symptoms like fever, unusual bleeding, or extreme
fatigue warrant prompt medical evaluation rather than watching and
waiting.
